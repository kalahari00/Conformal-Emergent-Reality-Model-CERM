A Paired Tidal Test of Ultralight Vector Dark Matter

Separating Source Abundance from Gravitational Response in the Conformal Emergent Reality Model Framework

Salman Akhtar · Independent Researcher · ORCID: 0009-0000-8105-9597

Preprint v1.0 · 13 November 2026

DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.24218678

Read full PDF Latest version on Zenodo →

Abstract

Ultralight dark matter can produce a periodic gravitational signal. Its amplitude mixes the amount of source matter with the response of geometry and cannot separate them. We show that a coherent vector supplies the missing observable. Its stress carries a fixed ratio of isotropic to directional components at one frequency, so the two curvature channels it drives calibrate each other and the unknown density cancels. Nonzero scalar–curvature mixing raises their normalized fast-response ratio above the Einstein–Proca value of unity, by an amount that measures how strongly the gravitational coupling varies with the scalar. We derive this result for a general canonical scalar with positive coupling and average it over an occupied scalar ensemble. The method therefore applies to a class of scalar–tensor theories with a minimally coupled vector source, not only to the model studied here.

We develop the method for the Conformal Emergent Reality Model, where the ratio measures the occupied variance of the retained scalar once its curvature coupling is specified independently. We evolve the coupled local state, bound the frequency and wavelength corrections, and derive a pulsar-timing template whose quadrupole-to-monopole coefficient ratio is eight times that response ratio. A common clock oscillation absorbs the monopole exactly—a degeneracy independent of the gravitational model—and matched delayed pulsar terms remove it in an ideal reconstruction. A benchmark lies about twelve times below the published Parkes limit for the ordinary-Proca template, and a conditional Fisher forecast gives the required precision and its sensitivity to endpoint matching.

Keywords: ultralight vector dark matter; scalar–tensor gravity; pulsar timing; gravitational response; CERM; Omegon.

Abstract

The deepest obstruction to unifying general relativity and quantum mechanics is usually called the problem of time, but it is equally a problem of space. Quantum mechanics requires a clock and a ruler supplied from outside the system, while general relativity makes both part of the dynamical geometry. Quantize the whole universe, and there is no outside left to hold either one.

In the Conformal Emergent Reality Model (CERM), both arise from the geometry itself. A single scalar conformal field Ω gives causal geometry physical scale through its local response to Weyl curvature and its accumulated curvature history, Chronos. Quantizing an internal clock and its conjugate curvature in a homogeneous reduction yields a quantum-geometric uncertainty principle. A related bound links the resolution of clocks and rulers. In the correspondence limits, the construction recovers Einstein gravity and proper-time quantum evolution. In the Standard Model extension, Chronos sets the fading mass scales. Its explicit factor cancels from the mass term of the conformally transformed Dirac equation, and ordinary local propagation is recovered in the correspondence limit. Departures from Higgs tracking yield a correlated neutrino response.

In CERM’s effective dynamics, inflation brings about its own end as rapid expansion weakens the curvature that sustains it. As galaxies and clusters form, local curvature sets the mass of the Omegon, an excitation of the same conformal field and a candidate for dark matter. In CERM’s late-time regime, the acceleration attributed to dark energy arises from the same geometric construction as Chronos accumulates. Chronos also changes the clocks and rulers used to measure expansion. This shared evolution underlies CERM’s proposed resolution of the Hubble tension. It gives the early acoustic ruler and the local distance ladder a common calibration for inferring today’s expansion rate.

When the aeon’s fading masses fall below the quantum-geometric resolution threshold, its geometry ceases to sustain physical clocks and rulers. The aeon ends, and only a structured boundary seed carries forward. CERM describes this passage by analytically continuing proto-time. The seed gives the next aeon a low-entropy beginning, with Weyl curvature suppressed and Ricci curvature at its finite maximum. This carries Penrose’s conformal cyclic picture into a crossover at finite scale.

The derivations, data comparisons, and literature context are developed in Unfreezing Time v1.1, Fading Mass v1.0, and Adaptive Dark Matter v1.0.
